# How can we design a survey that captures meaningful differences across contexts?

## Research question

How can questionnaire design, pilot testing, and sampling be combined to develop a survey that produces useful and reliable data across different contexts?

## About the project

This project documents an applied survey research workflow covering questionnaire development, pilot testing, sampling, and quantitative analysis.

The project is based on work conducted in an international education research context. Sensitive, confidential, or proprietary materials are not included in this repository.

## Research approach

The project follows the research process from design to pilot:

1. Defining the research questions
2. Developing the questionnaire
3. Considering measurement and response options
4. Designing the sampling strategy
5. Conducting a pilot
6. Examining pilot results
7. Using the findings to inform further improvements

## What I worked on

The project demonstrates experience with:

- questionnaire development
- survey methodology
- sampling strategy
- pilot research
- quantitative data analysis
- research documentation
- communicating methodological decisions

## Why the pilot matters

Pilot testing provides an opportunity to identify potential issues with the questionnaire and research process before the main data collection.

The analysis therefore focuses not only on the results themselves, but also on what the pilot can reveal about the quality and feasibility of the research design.
